# Child Mortality Trends 1900–2020

Longitudinal analysis of U.S. child mortality across four pediatric age 
groups (ages 1–4, 5–9, 10–14, and 15–19) spanning 120 years. The 
analysis documents the long structural decline in child mortality and 
isolates historically anomalous periods for visual examination.

---

## Key Findings

Child mortality declined consistently across all four age groups from 
1900 to 2020. The most pronounced anomaly in the data is the 1918 
influenza pandemic, which produced a sharp mortality spike - most visible 
in the younger cohorts - before the long-term downward trend resumed.

The 1–4 age group shows the steepest overall decline, consistent with 
20th-century advances in sanitation, vaccination, and pediatric care.

---

## Visualizations

- Annotated line plots by age group with confidence intervals
- Bar and categorical plots for cross-group comparison
- KDE, ECDF, and histogram plots for distributional analysis
- Subplots isolating selected year ranges for period-specific examination
- Box plots for spread and outlier identification across decades
